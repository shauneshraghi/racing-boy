# Racing Boy

A browser kart-style racing game in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step and no runtime dependencies (`package.json` holds only scripts and `playwright-core` for headless screenshots), a sibling of Dig Through Worlds (`~/dig` locally, https://github.com/shauneshraghi/dig-through-worlds) in look and code style. A kid driver walks a garage plaza (garage for upgrades, paint shop, car lot, track signpost) and races five rivals on hand-made tracks seen from a chase camera behind the car, in the style of 16-bit "Mode 7" kart racers (the plaza stays isometric). Races last 2–3 minutes (3 laps on shorter tracks, 2 on longer ones); there are ground boost pads, ramps with an air trick, drifting with a mini-boost, rare item crates and coins on the road. Only a 1st place opens the next track; winning a world's three tracks opens the next world. The agreed design is in `.scratch/spec.md`.

## Layout

- Graphics are real 3D by default (the user's kid asked for realistic graphics): three.js r158 vendored at `js/vendor/three.min.js` (MIT, `three.LICENSE`, the UMD build so `file://` still works), `js/models3d.js` (`RB3DModels`: PBR cars per `CARS` model and look, each driven by the kid (`driver()`: the plaza kid seated and posed, the wheel or handlebars placed at the hands; rivals pass a `RIDERS` palette from `render3d.js`, parked cars `false` for nobody; the closed cars are open-tops (`openTop`) and the van has a sunroof so the kid always shows), glider, treasure `chest`, shield, crate, coin, bee, goo, the kid, studio `preview` canvases, `envMap`), `js/world3d.js` (`RB3DWorld`: race scenes from track geometry with road ribbon, kerbs, pads, ramps matching the logic's profile, creeks, walls, terrain, instanced trees and grass, per-world sky and light, hazard models; the plaza from `HUB.rows`. On hilly tracks `Field.ry` carries the road's height out over the land, so `Field.height` and everything placed with it sit on the hills, and strips, walls and ramps add `RB.groundAt`) and `js/render3d.js`, which replaces `RBRender` with the same API (chase camera close behind so the kid reads (`CAM`), sun shadows following the player, bloom and boost blur on desktop, pooled particles, overlay text). It falls back to the pixel renderer below with `?gfx=pixel`, `?hd=0`, or no WebGL. `tools/models.html` shows every model (`?focus=<name>&view=front|rear|side|top|low&solo=1`, `?previews=1`). `?bloom=0|1` overrides bloom.
- `index.html` is the page shell. It loads the scripts in order with plain `<script>` tags (logic, post, sprites, render, audio, ui), so the game also runs from `file://`. The stylesheet and scripts carry a `?v=N` stamp: bump it on every deploy, because GitHub Pages lets browsers keep each file for 10 minutes.
- `js/logic.js` is the pure game logic (`RB`), no DOM: tracks, car physics, items, rival AI, economy, unlocks, the plaza map and walking, and save/load. Tested in Node.
- `js/post.js` is the HD-2D post-process (`RBPost`), grown from Dig's: WebGL2 upscaling with hard pixels, depth of field (a screen band on the plaza, a depth buffer in races), distance fog, drifting cloud shadows, sun shafts, screen-space point lights, bloom, grading (a low-key tone curve `tone` = [gamma, exposure], warm highlights, cool shadows, vignette, grain) and, while boosting, a radial speed blur with a colour fringe. Without WebGL2 (or with `?hd=0`) the renderer draws plain 2D.
- `js/sprites.js` is the pixel art (`RBSprites`). Cars are box models (`carBoxes`) rendered per heading (32 headings) into small canvases, hard-edged, cached per car, look and heading; paint, trim, wheels, spoiler and exhaust are boxes in the model. Outlines (`crisp`) are a darkened shade of the colour they border, not black. Buildings, the windmill, the grandstand and the item crate are models too: `mbox` gives a box materials (`material`: brick, tile, slate, plank, stone, stucco, strata, overlays mapped onto each face's parallelogram by `texFace`), `roof` is a gabled roof, and `houseBoxes` builds a house from a `HOUSES` entry (plinth, textured walls, tiled roof with eaves, shuttered windows, flower boxes, door, chimney). Trees and bushes (`FOL`, `foliage`) and the canyon's cacti, boulders and hoodoos (`SOLID`, `solid`) are drawn per pixel at the height they are shown (`scenery(name, px)`, snapped to `FOL_PX`): leaf clumps lit from the upper left in five tones. The kid (`KID`, `KID_LEGS`, `KID_PAL`: front, back and a mirrored side view, 16×27 with three tones a material, legs in a standing and two walk frames) and the small props (posts, tyres, hay, sign, palm) are letter grids recoloured by palettes. New chase-camera views are drawn within a per-frame budget (`newFrame`, `BUDGET` ms); past it a missing size borrows the nearest one already drawn.
- `js/render.js` is the renderer (`RBRender`). A track's ground is one top-down texture (`TEX` = 12 texels per cell: grass, rough, road, curbs on bends, centre line, checker start, creeks, boost pads).
  - Races use a chase camera (`CAM` tuning block: distance behind, follow rate, draw distance, and a landscape and an upright-phone layout giving buffer width, focal length and the horizon and car rows). The scene is drawn into a small buffer (320 px wide, 240 upright) and scaled up with hard pixels: the ground is sampled per pixel from the texture and three smaller copies (`groundData`), the sky is a gradient with far hills and a near tree line with farms that scroll as the camera turns (`backdrop`), and everything standing up goes through `proj(x, y, z)` and is drawn far to near. Cars, buildings, crates and the glider are box models seen from the camera (`RBSprites.carView`, `modelView`, `gliderView`, cached per angle, elevation and a fixed set of sizes); trees, posts and hay are letter-grid billboards (`BILL_H` heights). Rivals and the finish arch between the camera and the player turn see-through.
  - HD-2D in races: alongside the buffer the renderer keeps a depth buffer (`lo.dcv`, red = sqrt(depth / `RBPost.DMAX`), 255 = open sky). Ground rows get their depth in `drawGround7`; each standing item's silhouette is stamped with its depth (`setDepth` before the item draws, then `stamp` in `bill` and `model`, and `poly7` fills). Particles, coins and motes are not stamped. The tilt-shift (`DOF` tuning block) keeps the car and the road to the next bends sharp and softens the distance and anything close to the camera; fog, cloud shadows (the post-process works out the world point under each pixel from `cam`) and sun shafts all read the same buffer. Each world's sky colours, haze, sun bearing, cloud colours and colour grade are in `SKY`, and its ground colours and scenery in `LAND`, both keyed by world id (`hills`, `desert`; other worlds fall back to `hills`). Near standing things spread their blur past their own silhouette (never onto the player's car), and scenery very close to the camera fades out. The grade aims at Square Enix's HD-2D look: low-key (a gamma above 1 sinks the mid-tones), warm golden key light, brown/teal shadows, earthy muted ground textures (tufted olive grass, worn asphalt, cobbles on the plaza), dappled light on the ground, dark woods framing the road, strong vignette and motes; the road out to the next bend stays sharp. `RBRender.SKY` and `RBRender.DOF` are exposed for trying values in the console. The sky has drifting pixel clouds and a sun, shadows fall away from the sun (`shadow7`), and pollen motes drift near the camera.
  - The plaza is isometric: its texture goes through an affine transform and sprites sort by x + z. With HD-2D the ambient is low and a warm light follows the kid, so the plaza reads as a pool of light framed by dark woods. `setView` keeps that camera centred beside an open panel.
  - Text (name tags, floaters, station icons) goes on the `#ov` overlay. The race minimap is top-down.
- `js/audio.js` is WebAudio (`RBAudio`): sound effects, an engine hum that follows speed, horns (a customisation slot), and original synth music in the style of 16-bit future racers: a theme per track (`setMood('race', trackId)`; unknown ids play meadow), a calmer plaza theme, results themes (`setMood('results', won)`) and a final-lap lift (`setIntensity`). Music and effects run through their own gains (volume sliders saved under `racing-boy-v1-vol`) into a compressor and limiter. Songs are data (bars of 16th-note strings, melodies in scale degrees, chords as Roman numerals; format comment above them) played by brass lead, pick or slap bass, palm-muted distorted chords, arpeggio, pad and drum voices through a shared stereo echo. `_render`, `_songs` and `_layout` are test hooks for rendering a theme offline.
- `js/ui.js` holds state, saving, the HUDs, plaza panels, race flow, input and the main loop, and sets `window.__game`.
- `tests/logic.test.mjs` and `tests/hub.test.mjs` run `js/logic.js` in a Node `vm` context.

## Tracks and racing

- A track (`TRACKS` in `logic.js`) is a closed list of control points in cells, scaled by `scale`, with a road half-width `hw`. `RB.track(id)` builds the geometry once: a Catmull-Rom centreline resampled every 0.5 cells (`samples` with tangent and curvature `k`), lap length `L`, and a nearest-sample grid for `query(x, z, hint)` → arc length `s`, signed offset `d` (+ is the driver's right) and the wall offsets there (`limR`, `limL`). With a hint (the asker's last `s`: every car, bee and render call passes one) it searches only the road within `HINT_N` samples of the hint, so at a crossover a car stays on its own level; without one it uses the grid. Features are placed by lap fraction `u` and offset: `pads`, `ramps` (`gap` > 0 puts a creek after the lip), item crate rows (`boxes`, three crates per row), coin lines and `hazards`. Keep separate parts of a track at least ~16 cells apart, except at a crossover.
- Crossovers, bridges and open edges: `bridges: [[u0, u1]]` or `[[u0, u1, 'rail']]` are decks (`g.deck` per sample, `RB.onBridge(g, s)`); a track may pass over itself only on one, `BRIDGE_CLEAR` (4.5) cells above the road below where their roads overlap, and within 16 cells of another stretch the higher one must be on a deck (tested for every track). A deck has no runoff: its edges are open unless it has a rail (a wall at `hw + RAIL`). `drops: [[u0, u1, side]]` open the wall on one side (1 right, -1 left, 0 both) over a cliff. Past `hw + EDGE` on an open side a car falls (`fall` event, `fellAt` -1, it carries on outward and drops) and is put back on the road 3 cells behind where it went over after `FALL_T`. Coins, crates, goo, bees and car bumps compare world heights (`gy + y`), so nothing reaches across levels. In 3D (`bridges()` in `world3d.js`) a deck is a concrete slab with piers down to the land (never onto a road below), a red and white lip along open edges, a steel rail on railed ones; `Field` builds the land from the ground road only (`g.queryLand`, `g.nearLand`), keeps it under a deck (`deckY`), carves it down toward each bridge's base (the lowest ground road within 40 cells, `carve`) so a high deck stands in open air, and drops it `LOOK.cliffDepth` (9) beside open cliff edges (`cliff`, fading out 40–110 cells past the walls so it reads as a drop, not a trench). `LOOK.deck: 'plank'` (the canyon) builds timber decks on trestle legs. Every track now has at least one crossover; the bots never fall off an open edge, so only players do. World 1–2 peaks stay at about 6 at most because the volcano test asks every volcano peak to be twice theirs. The minimap draws bridges on top, outlined. Design a track with `node tools/track-plot.mjs <id>` (lap, heights, every crossing and near miss, bridge and drop spans, a clean stock-bot time and fall count, and a PNG of the layout coloured by height in `output/`) and `node tools/track-u.mjs <id> '[[x,z],...]' scale` (the lap fraction of each control point of a draft layout).
- Hills: `elev` keys (`[u, height]`, joined by smoothstep S-curves) give every sample a ground height `y` and slope `gy` (`RB.groundAt`, `RB.slopeAt`); the ground is level across the road. `car.y` is the height above the ground under the car and `car.gy` that ground's height (renderers draw at `gy + y`); `car.vy` is the true vertical speed. Climbing slows the top speed by `SLOPE_K` × slope; where the ground falls away faster than gravity (a short steep drop after a rise, at speed) the car flies off the crest (`jump` event with `crest`), and a trick there boosts like a ramp. A run of short humps is a set of whoops; a pad before a crest means big air. Keep jump gaps on level ground. Each track is built around set pieces (see the comment above each in `TRACKS`); `.claude/skills/drift-tune` and a plot of the layout are the checks when editing one.
- Hazards (`HAZ`: sheep, bale, tumble, boulder; snowball, penguin, iceblock; bomb, magma, vent; rover, moonrock, meteor and the dish robot in the later worlds) cross the road and back on a sine of `period` s with amplitude `hw + HAZ_OVER`; `n` makes a flock spaced `gap` apart. `RB.hazardAt(r, i)` is pure in race time (position, heading, hop, roll). Touching one is `hit(r, car, 'hazard')`: a short slow (`HAZ_SLOW`), blocked by a shield, then `HAZ_COOL` s of grace. Rivals and the clean bot steer round them (`dodge`); rivals miss one now and then. The 3D renderer draws them (`hazardObj` in `world3d.js`); the pixel fallback draws neither hills nor hazards.
- Worlds: Grassy Hills Town, Sunbaked Canyon, Snowcap Peaks (`ice`), Lava Lane (`volcano`), Moon Base (`moon`), three tracks each. A world can set environment factors (`grip` × turning and tyre bite: ice 0.85; `grav` × gravity: the moon 0.55, so air lasts 2–3 s and chasm gaps are much wider; `rough` × the off-road factor: volcanic ash 0.85; `RB.envOf`), and a track's `ai` multiplies the rivals' `aiSpeed`, so difficulty ramps through a world as well as between worlds (rival `aiSpeed` 8.8, 9.6, 10.6, 11.7, 12). The later worlds climb far higher (peaks of 10–16 cells against 6 at most in the first two, where bridges need the height) through switchbacks, spirals and crater walls. A vent hazard (`HAZ.vent`) stays in its lane and only hits while puffing (`hz.on`). Each world's entry gate is its own test file (`tests/ice.test.mjs` at upgrade level 4, `volcano` at 5, `moon` at 6) and is checked with the drift lab at that level (`--world 3 --up 4` and so on).
- In `world3d.js` a world's land, scenery, walls, buildings and far land come from its `LOOK` entry (`LOOK_DOC` lists the keys) and the registries `HILLS`, `MTN`, `BUILD`, `WALLS`, `FAR`, `HAZOBJ` and `PROTOS`; add a world there, not with `worldId ===` branches.
- Off the road is rough grass for `RUNOFF` cells, then a wall that bounces the car back. Landing short in a creek puts the car back on the road past it after `FALL_T`.
- The logic runs at a fixed `DT` of 1/60 s with a seeded rng (`startRace` seeds from `s.seed` and `s.races`), so bot tests are deterministic. `RB.input(r, inp)` takes `steer` (−1..1, car-relative: keys and the ◀ ▶ buttons on desktop, which set `ease` so the steering builds over `STEER_RISE` (a tap is a nudge; `DRIFT_RISE` in a drift), and on a phone a joystick that appears under a thumb on the left half of the screen, with a deadzone, travel and curve: `STICK_DEAD`, `STICK_FULL`, `STICK_CURVE` in `ui.js`; a thumb pushed past full lock drags the stick's centre along, so easing back unwinds at once). Outside a drift `steer` goes through a curve (`STEER_LIN` × s + (1 − `STEER_LIN`) × s³) so small inputs correct gently and full lock keeps the hairpin turn; `aim` is not curved, and the sloppy test bot inverts the curve (`unshape`) or `aim` (a world heading, used by the bots), plus `drift` (held: keys, the drift button, or on a phone any finger on the right half; a press on the ground hops at once for `HOP_T` (steering turns the car only `HOP_STEER` × grip in the hop) and the drift starts on landing if steering, then keeps that direction until let go, turning `DRIFT_WIDE`–`DRIFT_TURN` × grip and charging `DRIFT_CHARGE_WIDE`–1 per second as the stick goes from against the drift to into it, and sliding outward (`DRIFT_SLIDE`); letting go pays the boost of the highest of three `DRIFT_TIERS`; there is no brake; a press in the air is a trick) and `item` (a press). Phones are told apart by the `(pointer: coarse)` media query in `style.css`.
- Steering has weight: the yaw rate follows the steering with a lag (`YAW_LAG` × √weight, × `DRIFT_LAG` in a drift, × `YAW_UNWIND` outside a drift when the steering eases off or reverses, so letting go stops the turn within a few degrees), and the car moves along `car.vh`, a direction of travel that swings toward the heading `car.h` at `GRIP_BITE` × grip / √weight per second (× `AIR_BITE` in the air, × `DRIFT_BITE` in a drift). `body()` in `logic.js` runs springs for the renderer: `roll` (leans out of a turn), `pitch` (squats under throttle), `dip` (compresses on landing) and `wheel` (front-wheel steer, counter-steering in a drift). `drawCar7` turns and squashes the car sprite about the ground by roll, pitch and dip at draw time, and `carView` bakes turned front wheels as a separate view only past ±0.35.
- Progress is `car.prog` (cells since the start, unwrapped). Race order sorts by it, and the lap is `floor(prog / L) + 1`; the race ends when the player finishes, and the rest of the field is finished at its present pace. The results screen has one way out: back to the garage, with the kid on its pad and the Garage panel open (`toGarage` in `ui.js`, `newHub('garage')`).
- Rivals (`aiInput`, `aiTop`) follow the centreline on their own lane (moved aside past hazards) at `WORLDS[w].aiSpeed × PACE × skill`, slow in sharp bends, and are rubber-banded toward the player (slowed up to 25% when ahead, sped up to 6% when behind). They use items too.
- Items (`ITEMS`): Rocket Pop (turbo), Triple Turbo (`turbo3`: `n` 3 uses, counted down in `car.itemN`), Bubble Shield, Coin Magnet, Goo Blob (a puddle that slows), Buzzy Bee (rare; chases the racer one place ahead). `rollItem` weights them by place; the leader never gets the bee or the triple turbo. Crates respawn after `BOX_RESPAWN` s; a car holds one item. The middle crate of each row is a golden treasure chest (`g.boxes[i].gold`): it rolls a little further back, never gives goo or the magnet, pays the player `CHEST_COINS` and returns after `CHEST_RESPAWN` s. A held item shows in a bubble over the driver's head (`overlayRace` in `render3d.js`: the roulette spins in it, a count badge for multi-use items) and in the big `#itemSlot` at the top of the race HUD (empty, rolling, then a pulsing ready box with the key to press; a tap uses it).
- Cars (`CARS`): the starter (`START_CAR`) is Zip, a motorbike (`model.bike`: two wheels, the 3D model leans into turns from `-roll` in `poseCar`) with Pip's stock stats so the balance tests hold; older saves get it free. Base stats, per-car upgrade caps, a perk (`PERKS`), a price, a box `model`, and a default look. Six starter cars can be bought at once; faster cars carry `unlock` (tracks to win first, `carUnlocked` / `carLockReason`) and rivals never drive them. `carStats` applies the six upgrades (`UP_KEYS`): Engine (top speed and pick-up), Turbo (pick-up and a `launch` boost at GO), Tires, Boost, Glider (`glide`: gentler fall, more air speed and steering, and a small boost on landing; the renderer opens a wing) and Armor. A new upgrade key needs a cap on every car; saves without it load at level 0. Upgrade costs rise by level, and every three levels need the next part tier (`PARTS`: bolts, gears, springs, chips, crystals; world w pays tier w, a win also one of tier w + 1, and the first win of each track a bonus of coins and parts of tier w + 1, tracked in `s.wins`). The garage's parts swap (`tradeParts`) trades `PART_UP` of a tier for one of the next and one for `PART_DOWN` of the tier below, so any track can farm the tier an upgrade needs.
- Cosmetics (`LOOKS`, slots `LOOK_KEYS`) are bought once (`s.looks`) and equipped per car (`s.cars[id].look`).

## Plaza

- `HUB.rows` is the map, one character per cell, x across and z down (key in the comment above it). Pads `g p c t` open the garage, paint shop, car lot and races panels. On the plaza, keys and the drag stick (touch only; a mouse drag does nothing) walk in screen directions; a tap or click walks there by the shortest path (`hubWalkTo`). After a panel is closed by ✕, the kid must step off the pad before it reopens (`padLock` in `ui.js`).

## Commands

- Run the tests with `node --test tests/*.test.mjs`. They print each track's race time and how often a clean and a sloppy bot win (the sloppy bot weaves, reacts at `BOT_REACT` and steers on the heading the car is winding toward; world 1 with the stock car, the desert with every upgrade at level 2, snow at 4, lava at 5 and the moon at 6: each world's entry gate).
- Tune drift handling with the `drift-tune` skill (`.claude/skills/drift-tune/`): `node .claude/skills/drift-tune/drift-lab.mjs [--set NAME=value,...] [--car id] [--world 2] [--up N]` drifts every bend of a world with a bot and reports how the drift arc fits them.
- Set up with `bash scripts/bootstrap.sh` (checks Node 20+, `npm install`, finds or fetches a Chrome; `--no-browser` skips that). README.md lists requirements.
- Play by serving the folder with `npm run serve` (`scripts/serve.mjs`, no caching) and opening `http://localhost:8766/`.
- Check a visual change headless with `npm run shot -- "<query>" [--size 390x844] [--wait s] [--step s] [--out output/x.png]` (`scripts/shot.mjs`: serves the repo on a free port, drives Chrome via `playwright-core`, prints page and console errors, and exits 1 if the page threw), then read the PNG. The default query is `race=meadow&dev=1&fresh=1&nosplash=1`. `--at u[,off]` puts the player at lap fraction u and starts racing there, `--look u,side,dist,height` fixes a camera beside the road at u looking at it (bridges and cliffs from outside), `--cam x,y,z,tx,ty,tz` a free one (`--look` straight sideways at a perpendicular crossing lands on the other road; use `--cam` there). Software WebGL is slow: allow 40 s to 3 min a shot (`--timeout`, default 300 s); web fonts are blocked.
- URL flags: `?dev=1` (lots of coins and parts, every track open, no saving), `?fresh=1` (new game, no load or save), `?nosplash=1`, `?seed=N`, `?hd=0` (plain 2D), `?race=<trackId>` (straight into a race), `?car=<carId>`.
- In the console, `window.__game` exposes `S`, `D` (the logic), `race`, `hub` and `step(seconds)`.

## Conventions

- Game rules and numbers live in `js/logic.js` and are tested first. Rendering sits on top and is not tested.
- The overall pace is `PACE` (top speed of every car and rival) and `PICKUP` (their acceleration) at the top of `logic.js`. A faster pace shortens races, so grow the track `scale`s with it, widen the jump `gap`s, and rerun the `drift-tune` lab: a drift's turn radius grows with speed.
- To change balance, edit `WORLDS` (`aiSpeed`, `skill`, `pay`), `CARS` (`base`, `caps`, `cost`), track `scale` and `laps`, `PLACE_PAY`, `PART_PAY`, `upgradeCost`, or the tuning block at the top of `logic.js`, then rerun the tests: world-1 races must last 2–3 minutes, a clean stock-car drive must win, and a sloppy one must sometimes win.
- Art is original and drawn in code. Do not copy names, characters, items or tracks from existing kart games.
- Never call `alert`, `confirm` or `prompt`. Start over asks for "yes" typed into an inline box.
- Saves go to `localStorage` under `racing-boy-v1`, every access wrapped in try/catch; the format is version 1 (`serialize`).

## Cloud sessions (Claude Code on the web)

- `.claude/settings.json` runs `.claude/hooks/session-start.sh` on startup and resume. When `CLAUDE_CODE_REMOTE=true` it runs `scripts/bootstrap.sh`, which uses the image's Chrome for Testing (`$CHROME_PATH`). The default Limited network blocks Playwright's browser CDN, so do not rely on `playwright install` there.
- The user can't open localhost from a cloud session: verify visuals with `npm run shot`, and hand play-testing over through GitHub Pages (https://shauneshraghi.github.io/racing-boy/, deployed from `main`; bump the `?v=N` stamps).
- The user's machine-level instructions don't reach the cloud. The ones that matter here:
  - The user is the only contributor and does not merge pull requests: commit to `main` and push it, with no PR, even when the session was started on a branch (then also push that branch, and fast-forward `main` to it; merge `main` in first if it has moved). Force pushes and history rewrites need the user's go-ahead.
  - Commits, comments and other repo artifacts carry no AI identity, session links or co-author trailers.
  - Game rules are test-first: write a failing test in `tests/`, then make it pass.
  - Reports to the user lead with the answer, plainly worded, with no recap or closing offer. Name things by what they are, not by run label or issue number alone.
  - Visual work aims at realistic 3D (PBR materials, soft shadows, natural light); the pixel HD-2D renderer is kept as a fallback. The art stays original.
